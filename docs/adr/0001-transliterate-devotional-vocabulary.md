# Transliterate the devotional vocabulary rather than translating it

The captions render devotional terms as themselves — **katha**, **sant**, **mandir**,
**shastra**, **Bhagvan** — rather than as their nearest English words. Manish settled this on
2026-09-01, asked directly whether the hall should read *Bhagwan or God, sant or saint, mandir
or temple*: "Bhagvan sant mandir shastra". He is the only person on the project who reads
Gujarati, so this is the one term decision with real authority behind it.

## Considered options

**Translate into English** — *scripture*, *saint*, *temple*. Reads more naturally to someone
with no exposure to the tradition, and the glossary originally did exactly this in places
(શાસ્ત્ર was "scripture"). Rejected: these words carry Christian-inflected connotations that
are wrong here, and a *sant* is not a *saint*.

**Transliterate throughout** — chosen. The words the sabha already uses for these ideas.

## Consequences

- The glossary must be internally consistent. It was not: `સંત` was already "sant" while
  `શાસ્ત્ર` was "scripture", and `ભગવાન` was spelt "Bhagwan" rather than his "Bhagvan". Mixed
  habits are worse than either policy, because they read as carelessness.
- **This does not settle every term.** `અવગુણ` is currently left untranslated as "avgun", and
  `મહાત્મ્ય` is Englished as "glory" — both are open, and neither should be changed by
  extending this decision on someone's own initiative. The ruling covers the four terms he
  named and the *instinct* behind them; it does not license guessing at the rest.
- There is a cost, and it is real: a reader who is following the English *because* they do not
  follow the Gujarati gains nothing from an untranslated term. That tension is why proper nouns
  and the remaining terms still need his eye (tracker #7).
